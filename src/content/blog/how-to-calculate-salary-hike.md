## Lesson 1: How is a Salary Hike Calculated? (The Formulas)

To compute your salary hike details, corporate HR departments use two primary models: percentage-based increases and fixed-amount raises.

### 1. Percentage Hike Formula
A percentage hike increases your base salary by a fraction of your current wage. This is the most common appraisal format:

$$\text{New Salary} = \text{Current Base Salary} \times \left( 1 + \frac{\text{Hike Percentage}}{100} \right)$$
$$\text{Hike Amount} = \text{Current Base Salary} \times \left( \frac{\text{Hike Percentage}}{100} \right)$$

For my $80,000 base salary and 10% target hike:

$$\text{New Salary} = \$80,000 \times 1.10 = \mathbf{\$88,000.00}$$
$$\text{Hike Amount} = \$80,000 \times 0.10 = \mathbf{\$8,000.00}$$

### 2. Fixed Hike to Percentage Conversion
If your employer offers a flat annual raise (such as a $5,000 increase) and you want to know the equivalent rate of return:

$$\text{Hike Percentage} = \left( \frac{\text{Hike Amount}}{\text{Current Base Salary}} \right) \times 100$$

Comparing a $5,000 raise to my $80,000 base salary:

$$\text{Hike Percentage} = \left( \frac{\$5,000}{\$80,000} \right) \times 100 = \mathbf{6.25\%}$$

Knowing this percentage is crucial. If the national inflation rate is averaging 6.0%, a $5,000 raise (6.25%) barely protects your purchasing power, while a 10% raise ($8,000) provides real, inflation-beating income growth.

To truly understand a pay raise, you must translate the new numbers across annual, monthly, and hourly intervals. 

In corporate human resources, full-time employment is calculated based on a **2,080-hour work year** (40 hours per week × 52 weeks).

## Lesson 3: Merit Raises vs. Cost-of-Living Adjustments (COLA)

When discussing salary hikes with your manager, it is vital to separate **merit raises** from **cost-of-living adjustments (COLA)**:

* **Cost-of-Living Adjustment (COLA):** A flat percentage raise applied across the entire company to match macroeconomic inflation (typically 2% to 4%). COLA is not a reward for performance—it simply keeps your salary's buying power stable so you don't take a "real wage cut" due to rising prices.
* **Merit Raise:** An increase in pay based on your individual performance, skill growth, and business contributions. Merit raises are where real wealth building happens.

If your employer offers you a 3% raise during a year when inflation was 4%, they are calling it a "raise," but you have actually suffered a **1% real wage decrease** in purchasing power. 

Always negotiate for merit increases *on top* of cost-of-living adjustments.

## 4 Steps to Negotiate a Higher Salary Hike Percentage

To prepare a professional business case and secure a larger raise during your appraisal cycle, follow these four rules:

### 1. Compile Your Value-Delivery Log
Do not walk into the meeting expecting your manager to remember everything you did over the past 12 months. Three weeks before the review, compile an itemized list of your key accomplishments, emphasizing:
* Projects delivered on time or under budget.
* Processes you automated or optimized to save team hours.
* Feedback from clients or cross-functional team leaders.

### 2. Quantify the Business Impact
Translate your accomplishments into concrete business numbers. Instead of saying "I helped improve client relations," say "I managed accounts that generated an extra $30,000 in revenue" or "I reduced ticket resolution times by 15%."

### 3. Benchmarking Local Market Compensation
Use salary resources like Payscale, Glassdoor, and LinkedIn Salaries to find the average pay scale for your title and experience level in your city. If market rates are higher than your current pay, share this data as an objective benchmark.

### 4. Focus on Value, Not Personal Expenses
Never base your negotiation on personal expenses (e.g. "I need a raise because my rent went up" or "I bought a house"). 

Employers pay for the value you deliver to the business, not your personal financial needs. Keep the conversation focused on your achievements, skill gains, and future commitments to the company.
