A few months ago, my partner and I bought a beautiful plot of rural land. 

Instead of building a traditional house from scratch, we decided to purchase a pre-owned, single-wide manufactured home. It was in excellent structural shape, but there was a catch:

**The home was located on a lot 80 miles away.**

We had to transport a 14-foot-wide, 70-foot-long structure across active state highways, narrow country roads, and over steep hills, and then install it onto our new land.

When I started calling transport companies for quotes, I was shocked by how varied the numbers were. 

One broker quoted a flat $3,000. Another quoted $9,500. 

I quickly realized that in the manufactured housing industry, there is no such thing as a "flat move fee." A mobile home move is an engineering and legal logistics project, and every service element carries its own cost multiplier.

To make sense of the quotes, I built a detailed spreadsheet and broke down the relocation into distinct cost centers:
* **Base Towing Fee:** The charge to hook up the home and haul it the first 50 miles.
* **Overage Mileage Surcharge:** The fee per mile traveled beyond the base distance.
* **Regulatory Permits:** State and local wide-load and oversize transportation permits.
* **Site Preparation & Setup:** Leveling the ground, laying block piers, and securing tie-down anchors.
* **Utility Hookups:** Reconnecting water, electrical, sewer, and testing plumbing.
* **Equipment Rentals:** Renting axles, tires, and towing tongues for transport.

Here is how the actual calculations for our **80-mile single-wide move** broke down:
* Base Transport (up to 50 miles): **$2,500.00**
* Mileage Surcharge (30 overage miles at $6.00/mile): **$180.00**
* Oversize Cargo Permits: **$200.00**
* Setup (Basic Blocking & Tie-downs): **$2,000.00**
* Utility Disconnect & Reconnect: **$1,000.00**
* Axles & Heavy-Duty Tires Rental: **$500.00**

Reconciling all these line items, our estimated total relocation cost came out to:

$$\text{Total Cost} = \$2,500.00 + \$180.00 + \$200.00 + \$2,000.00 + \$1,000.00 + \$500.00 = \mathbf{\$6,380.00}$$

Seeing this detailed breakdown helped us understand exactly where our money was going, evaluate transport quotes with confidence, and avoid hidden broker fees.

In this article, I want to outline the logistics and mathematics of mobile home moving, explain how width and distance impact permit costs, and share money-saving site prep tips to secure your relocation margins.

## Lesson 1: How Width & Size Determine Moving Costs

In manufactured home logistics, **width is the primary cost driver**. 

A single-wide home (typically 12 to 15 feet wide) can be towed as a single unit on standard lanes. 

However, double-wide (20 to 30 feet wide) and triple-wide (30+ feet wide) manufactured homes cannot be towed in one piece. They must be structurally split in half (or thirds), wrapped in protective plastic sheeting, hauled separately by two or three semi-tractors, and then structurally re-joined and sealed on-site.

This structural split doubles the workload, which is why transport rates scale progressively:
* **Single-Wide:** Base transport = $2,500 (includes 50 miles) + $6.00/mile overage.
* **Double-Wide:** Base transport = $5,000 (includes 50 miles) + $12.00/mile overage.
* **Triple-Wide:** Base transport = $8,000 (includes 50 miles) + $18.00/mile overage.

## Lesson 2: Regulatory Surcharges & Escort Pilot Cars

Because manufactured homes are classified as oversize cargo, you cannot simply hook them up and drive. 

State departments of transportation require legal permits to move cargo that exceeds width and weight limits.

### 1. Oversize Load Permits
Every municipality and state line crossed requires a separate oversize load permit. 

Permit costs range from **$150 to $700** depending on the home’s dimensions, weight, and local administrative filing fees.

### 2. Pilot Escort Vehicles
If your cargo exceeds a certain width (often 12 feet), state laws mandate hiring **escort pilot vehicles** (cars equipped with warning signs, flags, and yellow flashing lights) to lead and follow the load.
* **Single-Wides:** Rarely require escort vehicles for local highway moves.
* **Double-Wides:** Legally mandate **1 escort vehicle** to follow the load (typically costing $2.00 per mile).
* **Triple-Wides:** Legally mandate **2 escort vehicles** (one in front, one behind) to clear oncoming lanes, costing around $4.00 per mile.

## Lesson 3: Foundation Blocking & Setup Types

Once the home arrives at the destination lot, it must be installed onto a foundation. 

There are two primary foundation options:

### 1. Basic Blocking & Tie-Downs
The setup crew places dry-stacked concrete blocks on steel ground pans underneath the home's steel chassis. They level the home using hydraulic jacks, place wooden shims, and secure the frame to the ground using heavy-duty steel anchors and strap tie-downs.
* *Single-Wide Cost:* **$2,000.00**
* *Double-Wide Cost:* **$4,000.00**
* *Triple-Wide Cost:* **$6,000.00**

### 2. Concrete Slab or Footing Piers
A contractor pours a solid concrete slab or concrete piers below the local frost line. The setup crew anchors the home directly into the concrete foundation. This setup is highly stable and required for FHA financing or in regions prone to severe winds.
* *Single-Wide Cost:* **$4,500.00**
* *Double-Wide Cost:* **$7,500.00**
* *Triple-Wide Cost:* **$10,500.00**

## Lesson 4: Utility Disconnect & Reconnect Requirements

A manufactured home move requires disconnecting and re-connecting four major utility systems:
1. **Electrical Service:** Wiring must be disconnected at the old lot and run to a new utility pole on-site.
2. **Plumbing & Water:** Fresh water lines and sewer pipes must be disconnected and re-piped.
3. **HVAC Systems:** Air conditioning units or heat pumps must be disconnected, transported, re-installed, and charged with refrigerant.
4. **Gas / Propane:** Fuel lines must be disconnected and pressure-tested upon re-installation.

Hiring licensed utility sub-contractors typically costs:
* *Single-Wide:* **$1,000.00**
* *Double-Wide:* **$1,800.00**
* *Triple-Wide:* **$2,500.00**

## Three Money-Saving Site Prep Tips

Site preparation is the easiest area to save money during a mobile home move. Follow these guidelines to reduce your costs:

### 1. Clear and Level the Lot in Advance
If the transport crew arrives at your new land and has to wait for trees to be trimmed, fences to be removed, or the ground to be cleared, they will charge you **$150 to $300 an hour** in wait-time fees. 

Ensure the path from the main highway to the foundation spot is completely clear and leveled.

### 2. Pre-dig Trenching for Utility Lines
Ask your utility hookup crew what trenching is required for electrical conduits and plumbing sewer lines. 

Digging these trenches yourself or hiring a local excavator before the moving crew arrives can save you hundreds of dollars in high-rate labor fees.

### 3. Consolidate Your Accessory Add-ons
If you are planning to install skirting or a new HVAC unit, check whether local contractors are cheaper than the transport company's add-on services. 

Often, local skirting installers offer better pricing than commercial transport companies who sub-contract the work out.
